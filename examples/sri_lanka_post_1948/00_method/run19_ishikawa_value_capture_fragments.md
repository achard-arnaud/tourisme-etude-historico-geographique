# Run19 — Ishikawa fragments: tea value capture

Status: fragments only; **not claims**. These fragments must be sanitized and sourced before promotion.

## Product
- Bulk auction leaf may remain weakly differentiated from the consumer point of view.
- Tea-origin protection must be checked precisely against the Lion certification mark and any GI/appellation regime.
- Blending may shift product identity and rent toward the blender rather than the origin.

## Process
- Green-leaf perishability plus road distance may create spatial monopsony where factory density is low.
- Factory ownership may be concentrated relative to leaf supply.
- Withering/firing energy may be a material volatile cost centre.

## Place / market structure
- Auction may discover price without transferring consumer-facing rent to origin.
- Consuming-market brands may own the shelf, recipe and customer relationship.
- Any tariff-escalation claim must be checked against current schedules.

## People
- Wage setting, attendance conditions and inherited supervision structures must be separated from productivity assumptions.
- Estate labour monopsony, leaf monopsony and buyer oligopsony are three different objects and require separate evidence.

## Policy
- Export levies, cess and input policy are candidate cost mechanisms.
- The 2021 agrochemical ban must be split into stated intent, operative foreign-exchange context and measured effect.
- The 53-year leasehold horizon versus replanting payback is a priority instrument question.

## Capital
- M3 tests whether company capital is deliberately allocated away from agronomy toward higher-return segments.
- Long-term agricultural credit and smallholder capital access are distinct constraints.

## Promotion gate
No fragment above becomes a claim until the value chain is decomposed by actor, value share and contestability, quantitative statements have full metric metadata, and M1/M2/M3 are tested against the preregistered falsifiers.
