## Causal question
Where in the tea chain is value created, and where is it retained?

## TL;DR
Three rival explanations are preregistered before claim promotion: structural commodity dependence, productive underperformance, and rational capital redeployment away from agronomy. The discriminating test is company capital allocation across a decade, read against segment returns and the leasehold/replanting horizon.

## Candidate mechanisms to test
- Green-leaf perishability + terrain + factory density → spatial monopsony over smallholders.
- Factory capital threshold and energy cost → concentrated processing chokepoints.
- Auction → price discovery without necessarily shifting consumer-facing rent to origin.
- Blending, branding and retail → ownership of customer relationship outside origin.
- Leasehold horizon vs replanting payback → potential disincentive to long-duration agronomic investment.
- Capital allocation across hospitality/property/branding/diversification → M3 only if company reports support it.

## Measurement discipline
Every value-share, production-share, replanting-rate, yield, wage, export-composition and perishability figure is a `metric` target with denominator, geography, period, basis and source definition. No circulating percentage enters the trunk without those fields.

## Internal comparators
- Ceylon cinnamon GI pathway versus tea protection.
- Packaged/value-added tea exports versus bulk exports.
- Orthodox versus CTC as production-strategy choices.
- Tea/cocoa/coffee only as bounded structural comparators with explicit confounders.
