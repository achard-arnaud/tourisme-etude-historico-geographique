# Stichting corpus — post-1948 routing

## Main-trunk relevance
- **Post-War Sri Lanka seminar (2023):** discovery hub for scholars on rebel governance, violence against women, reconciliation and Rajapaksa-era populism/authoritarianism. It does not itself substantiate those claims.
- **Restitution / Who Owns Heritage / Whose Law?:** relevant when Dutch–Sri Lankan bilateral relations, provenance law and decolonisation of collections enter the narrative.

## HIL-08 / memory and historiography
- **Invented Heritage — last Kandyan king’s “prison cell”:** strong example of a popular monument narrative contradicted by archival evidence. Useful for explaining how postcolonial societies manufacture legible heritage symbols. The proposed 1950s indigenisation motive remains a hypothesis.
- **Galle under threat:** turns UNESCO recognition into a political-economy mechanism: land values, investor demand, tourism and resident displacement.
- **Towards a Virtual Slave Island:** connects VOC-era forced migration and urban settlement to present-day redevelopment, eviction and the politics of whose city gets preserved.
- **Jaffna Fort:** the fort’s destruction during the civil war and postwar conservation make a colonial monument part of a much newer memory/reconciliation landscape.

## Bounded conclusion
The site is unusually valuable for the **bridge between colonial history and contemporary heritage politics**. It should not become the anchor for macroeconomics, electoral politics or the causes of the civil war merely because those themes also appear in Foundation events.
