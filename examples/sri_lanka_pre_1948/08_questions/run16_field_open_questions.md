# Run16 — open questions (Mihintale / Abhayagiri)

1. Which early Brahmi cave inscriptions at Mihintale independently attest monastic occupation, and what is their secure palaeographic range? **Run20 partial resolution:** Coningham et al. provides securely contextualized cross-dates between early Anuradhapura Brahmi finds and named Mihintale cave donors, supporting a third–second-century BCE epigraphic horizon and monastic donation context. A complete inscription-by-inscription Mihintale table is still required before closure.
2. Can any third-century-BCE element of the Mahinda sequence be anchored independently of the Mahavamsa tradition? **Still open:** early epigraphy independently anchors monastic occupation/donors, not the full Mahinda narrative.
3. How should BE 236 / BE 284 be displayed alongside mainstream academic chronologies without silently choosing one conversion?
4. Does the toponym Ambasthala have independently datable evidence connecting it to the mango-riddle episode, or only later textual tradition?
5. What do excavation reports for Kantaka Cetiya and the Mihintale medical complex establish about institutional chronology?
6. What archaeological report documents the 2024 Mihindu Seya restoration itself, beyond the commissioning stele?
7. What is the evidentiary status of the stele's 'enemy destruction' explanation for the ruin sequence?
8. **Review 2026-09-19:** have laboratory/technical analyses of the seventeen Abhayagiri bronzes been published, and do they support the provisional seventh-eighth-century stylistic dating?
9. Do the bronzes carry iconographic or technical traits that can distinguish Mahayana/Vajrayana affiliation from a generic Buddhist-art attribution?
10. How should Abhayagiri's trans-Asian connections be reconstructed without letting a 2026 discovery announcement close a long-running historiographic argument?
11. Can the Mahinda-IV slabs be tied securely to the same institutional space intended by later Mihintale narratives, and what discontinuities intervene?

Run16 policy: these questions outnumber the promoted claims by design. Ancient chronology remains open unless material evidence closes it.

New academic anchors may narrow the date and institutional envelope, but they do not retroactively convert chronicle episodes into source facts. The Abhayagiri technical-review gate remains scheduled for 2026-09-19.
