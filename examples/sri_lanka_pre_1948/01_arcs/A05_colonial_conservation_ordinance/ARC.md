# A05 — Colonial conservation and the 1937 ordinance

- Entry rupture: hunting/game protection becomes an organized colonial administrative concern in the late nineteenth century.
- Causal question: which institutions and instruments actually shaped the protected-area regime inherited after independence?
- Primary HILs: institutions/power; geography/environment; society/demography; economy/infrastructure.
- Comparative rule: resemblance to Yellowstone, Kruger, India or Thailand is not genealogy unless a transmission channel is evidenced.
- Exit rupture: the 1937 Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance enters into force in 1938 and provides the statutory architecture subsequently inherited and amended by the post-1948 state.
- Related post-1948 arc: A08_heritage_memory_governance.
