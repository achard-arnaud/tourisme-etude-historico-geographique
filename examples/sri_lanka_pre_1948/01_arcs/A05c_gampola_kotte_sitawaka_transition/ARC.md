# A05c — Gampola, Kotte and Sitawaka transition

evidence_status: shell

## Entry rupture

The narrative leaves the mobile-capital phase and follows the concentration of political, monastic and literary activity through Gampola and Kotte before the sixteenth-century fragmentation around Sitawaka and direct Portuguese pressure.

## Causal question

How did shifts in court location, monastic patronage, literary production and Indian Ocean/Portuguese pressure reshape the Sasana and elite culture between the fourteenth and sixteenth centuries?

## Evidence lanes to build

- Gampola temple and monastic patronage: Niyamgampaya, Gadaladeniya, Lankatilaka.
- Dharmakirti lineages and Sandesha literature.
- Kotte under Parakramabahu VI: court patronage, verse culture and transregional ordination links.
- Sitawaka: political fragmentation, Rajasingha I traditions, and the claimed decline of Pali/Sanskrit literary production.
- Portuguese intervention must be separated into military, political, missionary and economic mechanisms rather than used as a single explanatory cause.

## Exit rupture / bridge forward

The arc should hand off separately to Portuguese maritime intervention and to the consolidation of the Kandyan kingdom, rather than treating Sitawaka as a simple prelude to Kandy.
