# A08 — Jaffna caste and colonial codification

## Entry rupture

Dutch and then British administrations increasingly record land, household status, service obligations and legal categories in Jaffna.

## Causal question

How did colonial registration and legal change rework existing hierarchy without treating caste as a European invention?

## TL;DR

`C-PRE-CASTE-001` to `C-PRE-CASTE-003` distinguish institutional legibility, formal emancipation and persistent social hierarchy. The thombo system could reproduce coercive hierarchy while also creating documentary arenas for negotiation.

## Drivers / amplifiers / consequences / non-causes

- Amplifier: `C-PRE-CASTE-001` — registration and codification could strengthen dominant control over subordinate groups.
- Constraint: `C-PRE-CASTE-002` — formal abolition did not instantly dissolve dependence or hierarchy.
- Driver: `C-PRE-CASTE-003` — thombos linked land, status, taxation and service obligations within a negotiated bureaucracy.
- Non-cause guardrail: Europeans did not create caste ex nihilo.

## What changes the optimum?

Legal status can change faster than labour relations, property dependence and social authority; documentary legibility can be both coercive and negotiable.

## Exit rupture / bridge forward

The fixture ends with a durable distinction between formal institutional reform and slower social transformation, a distinction reused in the post-1948 corpus.
