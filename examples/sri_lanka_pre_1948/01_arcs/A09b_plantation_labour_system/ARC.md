# A09b — Plantation labour system

evidence_status: partial

- span: 1830–1948

## Causal question
How was a resident plantation workforce constructed, and what made the labour system survive the crop that first assembled it?

## TL;DR
The arc is not a generic labour-history bucket. It tests how recruitment, debt, employer-linked housing, gendered work allocation and difficult valley access made labour immobility durable across the coffee→tea transition. The intake's premise correction is binding: unions existed; the research object is institutional dependence and later dual union/political brokerage, not union absence.

## Separations
Imported labour recruitment/debt, Kandyan land alienation, line-room housing and later citizenship status are distinct mechanisms. Gendered plucking is treated as labour organization rather than timeless culture.

## Candidate mechanisms to test
- Recruitment and debt-based labour import → resident estate population.
- Tea's year-round work → higher value of retaining resident labour than under seasonal coffee.
- Line-room housing → employment contract coupled to tenancy and welfare access.
- Relief and single-access valleys → local labour-market monopsony rather than a national labour market.
- Siting of line rooms on marginal/unplantable land → potential exposure to unstable slopes; this remains an inference until hazard overlay and mortality evidence exist.

## Required evidence before promotion
- recruitment and estate-population series;
- dates and tenure status of line-room housing;
- alternative-employer access / commuting geography;
- estate versus non-estate hazard exposure;
- contemporary or colonial evidence separating labour retention from crop biology and ownership transition.

## Exit rupture / bridge forward
Citizenship legislation, nationalisation, privatisation and wage regulation carry the labour regime into `A18_malaiyaha_tamils_status_and_wage`.
