# A06 — VOC coastal state

## Entry rupture

The VOC–Kandy alliance against Portugal opens a transition from coalition warfare to Dutch possession of strategic coastal nodes. The rupture is not simply Portuguese → Dutch: the political problem changes when an ally that was hired to remove a maritime rival decides that the conquered ports are worth retaining.

## Problem-first causal question

**How can a commercial company turn ports captured on behalf of an inland ally into a durable coastal state — and what must it learn to control once forts alone are no longer enough?**

This question is form-global for the arc. Narration should proceed from the 1638–1658 alliance/conquest sequence to the local mechanisms of port control, then to administrative legibility, labour and production, before dezooming to wider VOC and European systems.

## TL;DR

The canonical spine is no longer just `forts + paper state`. It is a sequence:

**Kandy seeks naval leverage → VOC captures coastal nodes → captured nodes become competing sovereignty → forts require revenue and information → thombos/litigation/categories make land, people and service more legible → cinnamon labour is regulated and intensified → systematic cultivation begins in the late 1760s → the coastal state becomes both more productive and more expensive to administer.**

The arc therefore explains how a maritime intervention becomes territorial government without implying that the VOC controlled all of Ceylon.

## Canonical claims and mechanisms

- `C-PRE-001` — the anti-Portuguese alliance did not restore Kandyan control of the conquered ports; it produced competing Dutch coastal sovereignty.
- `C-PRE-002` — registration, litigation and social categorisation increased colonial legibility of land, people and service obligations.
- `C-R41-VOC-CINNAMON-CORVEE-001` — cinnamon production relied substantially on regulated corvée obligations among chialia/chalea groups; burdens generated resistance and flight.
- `C-R41-VOC-SYSTEMATIC-CULTIVATION-001` — systematic cinnamon cultivation began under the VOC in the late 1760s; British rule inherited an already-emerging cultivated system.
- `C-R45-MALAY-EXILE-DIASPORA-001` — Ceylon also became a node of coerced imperial mobility linking the Indonesian archipelago, soldiers, exiles, convicts and enslaved people to the island.

## Geography must do causal work

The coastal state is not a necklace of equivalent forts. Each node controls a different geography:

- **Mannar/Mantai** — trans-strait interface, resources and South-Indian connectivity;
- **Colombo/Galle** — export, administration and concentration of fiscal-commercial flows;
- **Trincomalee** — naval-strategic value at the Bay of Bengal/Coromandel scale;
- **Batticaloa** — lagoon/internal-interface function: protected local circulation, islands and a coast–hinterland checkpoint.

`B-R46-BATTICALOA-LAGOON-PORT-001` is therefore core-compatible. The fort should be narrated only after the lagoon and the circulation it concentrates.

## 360 viewpoints

### Kandyan monarchy
Objective: use Dutch naval power to break Portuguese coastal control and recover access to the sea.

Constraint: limited oceanic naval projection and dependence on externally controlled ports.

### VOC
Objective: convert expensive military conquest into defensible revenue, monopoly and administrative capacity.

Constraint: garrison, administration, smuggling, local resistance and the continuing independence of Kandy.

### Intermediaries
Headmen, interpreters, merchants, local officials and Christian/Eurasian communities make the coastal state operable. They are not passive transmission belts; they bargain, litigate and translate between regimes.

### Producers / service groups / mobile populations
Cinnamon peelers, households registered in thombos, coerced migrants and exiles experience the coastal state through obligations, categorisation, litigation and mobility rather than only through high diplomacy.

## Successive-regime inheritance

The arc must test what survives regime change rather than imply replacement.

- Portuguese fortifications and administrative practices are reused or transformed.
- Local land/service structures are not invented ex nihilo by the VOC but are rendered more legible and enforceable in Dutch documentary institutions.
- Christian and Eurasian/Burgher communities persist across Portuguese, Dutch and British sovereignties; `B-R46-BURGHER-COLONIAL-LAYERS-001` is the bridge for intermediary capital and social persistence.
- British rule in 1796 inherits not an empty coast but forts, law, records, cultivated cinnamon, categories and people experienced in colonial administration.

## Intent → instrument → friction → adaptation → unintended effect

Primary pattern for the arc:

**commercial monopoly / secure ports → forts, treaties, registers, labour regulation → Kandyan resistance, flight, smuggling, information limits and administrative cost → deeper documentary and productive intervention → a company becomes a territorial state and produces institutions/communities that outlive it.**

## Guardrails / unresolved points

- Do not turn Dutch coastal sovereignty into control of the whole island.
- Do not describe thombos as neutral census photography or as a uniform invention of fixed identities.
- Do not describe VOC use of chialia/chalea service as creating caste ex nihilo.
- Do not claim the British invented systematic cinnamon cultivation after 1796.
- Do not treat every port as performing the same function.
- Do not project the later British census category `Burgher` unchanged backward into the Portuguese or early VOC periods.
- Batticaloa must not be promoted into a major oceanic entrepôt without specific trade evidence.

## What changes the optimum?

The VOC optimum is reached when coastal possession can be converted into enough revenue, information and productive control to make a territorial state worthwhile without annexing the Kandyan interior. Its weakness is that each additional layer of sovereignty also creates costs of garrison, coercion, administration and conflict.

## Exit rupture / bridge forward

By the late eighteenth century, the Dutch coast is a dense military-documentary-commercial system, while Kandy remains sovereign inland. European wars then change the external balance: Britain can acquire the Dutch apparatus and combine it with a larger Indian fiscal-military system. The next problem is no longer simply `how to hold the coast?` but **whether the old coast/interior duality can survive a power able to integrate both.**
