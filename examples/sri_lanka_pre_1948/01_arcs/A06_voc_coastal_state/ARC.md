# A06 — VOC coastal state

## Entry rupture

The VOC–Kandy alliance against Portugal opens a transition from coalition warfare to Dutch control of strategic coastal nodes.

## Causal question

How did possession of ports and forts become a more territorial, fiscal and documentary form of rule?

## TL;DR

`C-PRE-001` and `C-PRE-002` form the canonical spine for this fixture: coastal sovereignty expanded beyond military occupation as legal, fiscal and classificatory instruments increased state legibility.

## Drivers / amplifiers / consequences / non-causes

- Driver: `C-PRE-001` — the anti-Portuguese alliance did not simply restore Kandyan control of the conquered ports.
- Driver: `C-PRE-002` — registration, litigation and social categorisation increased colonial legibility.
- Guardrail: do not turn negotiated categorisation into a claim that the VOC uniformly invented fixed identities.

## What changes the optimum?

The balance changes when coastal military possession creates administrative obligations and incentives for taxation, registration and legal control.

## Exit rupture / bridge forward

The Dutch coastal system becomes part of a wider Indian Ocean strategic order; later European wars alter the military alternatives available around Ceylon.
