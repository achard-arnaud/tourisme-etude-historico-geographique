# A07 — European wars and regional reordering

## Entry rupture

The eighteenth-century Anglo-French contest in South Asia changes the regional balance in which Ceylon's European and Kandyan actors operate.

## Causal question

How did successive European wars reshape strategic alternatives without collapsing distinct conflicts into one teleological story?

## TL;DR

`C-PRE-GEO-001` to `C-PRE-GEO-004` separate four mechanisms: Anglo-French strategic asymmetry, the earlier Portuguese decline, the 1796 British seizure of Dutch Ceylon, and the weaker external-balancing environment surrounding the 1815 annexation of Kandy.

## Drivers / amplifiers / consequences / non-causes

- Driver: `C-PRE-GEO-001` — the long Anglo-French contest, not one isolated war.
- Context: `C-PRE-GEO-002` — Portuguese marginalisation predates the Seven Years War.
- Driver: `C-PRE-GEO-003` — Dutch Ceylon passed to Britain during the Revolutionary/Napoleonic cycle before 1815.
- Amplifier: `C-PRE-GEO-004` — reduced Dutch/French alternatives weakened Kandy's external balancing; internal political fracture remains the immediate path to annexation.

## What changes the optimum?

British fiscal-military and naval advantages become regionally decisive when rival European positions are weakened or aligned against Britain.

## Exit rupture / bridge forward

By 1815 Britain controls the coasts and faces a Kandyan polity with reduced external-balancing options; the next problem is how colonial institutions reorganise society and obligation within the island.
