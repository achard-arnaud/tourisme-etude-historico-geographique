# Drift audit — Dutch Forts exhibition

## Vertical biases
- **Fort/architecture survivorship**: masonry is visible; village labour, inland traders and informal routes are less visible.
- **VOC archive bias**: fiscal and legal categories make subjects legible through colonial needs.
- **elite intermediary bias**: translators and clergy such as the de Melho family are unusually recoverable compared with ordinary workers.

## Horizontal biases
- **coast versus Kandy binary**: useful at strategic scale, misleading if read as economic autarky. Goods and people continued to cross frontiers.
- **European succession teleology**: Portuguese → Dutch → British can erase Kandyan, Tamil, Muslim and Indian Ocean agency.
- **fort = control**: a fortified node creates capacity, not automatic territorial obedience.

## Semantic drifts to block
- “ally” → “liberator”: false. The alliance was instrumental and interests diverged.
- “cinnamon motive” → “single-cause colonialism”: too narrow once taxation, law, labour and intra-Asian trade enter the system.
- “slave” → every form of coerced labour: false. Slavery, caste obligation, debt bondage and rajakariya overlap but are not synonyms.
- “Kandy excluded from world trade” → literal isolation: overstatement. Read as an attempt at coastal monopoly and strategic enclosure.
