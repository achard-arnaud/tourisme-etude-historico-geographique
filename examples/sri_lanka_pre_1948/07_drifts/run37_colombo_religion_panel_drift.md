# Run37 — historiography drift audit: Colombo religion panels

## Drift 1 — `Vaitulya = Mahayana` as a one-to-one identity
The museum panel is useful for national historiographical synthesis, but modern scholarship warns against treating polemical Pali labels as stable sect names with fixed doctrinal content. Preserve the stronger proposition — Abhayagiri was doctrinally plural and contested — while bounding the taxonomy.

## Drift 2 — named Vajrayana transmitters as a closed chain
Gunavarman, Vajrabodhi and Amoghavajra are historically important transregional Buddhist figures, but Run37 does not yet hold source-specific evidence sufficient to assert the exact Sri Lanka transmission chain printed by the panel. Retain as research leads.

## Drift 3 — `major religion` / `permanent stability`
The Hinduism panel's closing formulation is not a quantitative historical variable. It collapses several scales: elite institutions, Tamil-speaking communities, ports, royal patronage and island-wide population. Run37 replaces it with a bounded claim that Chola rule amplified/institutionalized Saiva influence while earlier Brahmanical/Saiva presence already existed.

## Drift 4 — generic `syncretism`
Two mechanisms must remain separate:
1. Anuradhapura/early-medieval coexistence: Buddhist monasteries and autonomous Brahmanical/Saiva sites/networks can coexist in the same political landscape.
2. Kandyan Perahera: selected Hindu deities are incorporated into a Buddhist royal-ritual hierarchy.
Calling both simply `syncretism` erases the mechanism.

## Drift 5 — bronze conflation
The panel's c. tenth-century Hindu bronzes and the newly reported 2026 Abhayagiri bronze find are not the same corpus. `C-R16-ABH-FIND-001` / `C-R16-ABH-DATE-001` remain provisionally dated modern finds; no backward inference from the museum panel is permitted.
