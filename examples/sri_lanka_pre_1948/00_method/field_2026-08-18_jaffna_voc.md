# Field evidence — Jaffna Fort, 18 August 2026

Tier: **T4 field mediation**. Underlying exhibition: **T2 specialist institutional anchor**.

## Panels captured
The on-site banner exhibition presents four particularly useful claims:

1. **Kandy–VOC alliance and reversal.** Raja Sinha II sought Dutch help against Portugal; after the Portuguese were expelled, the VOC retained the conquered coast rather than handing it to Kandy.
2. **Fort chain as territorial system.** A 1751 map displays twenty-two forts. The text assigns them several roles: deterrence of European rivals, bases for patrols and coercion, and a barrier against Kandyan campaigns.
3. **Commercial enclosure.** The exhibition emphasizes cinnamon, elephants and areca nut exports and imports of Indian textiles. Its rhetoric that Kandy was a “prisoner” and gained nothing from international trade is treated as an interpretive claim, not literal fact.
4. **Mannar as mixed military/economic node.** The panel links the fort to the coastal road, elephant movement toward Jaffna, choya dye roots for Indian textiles, intermittent pearl fisheries, and enslaved labour used in fort maintenance.

## Sanitization
- “VOC replaced Portuguese coastal sovereignty after helping Kandy” → B, strong and independently corroboratable.
- “whole enterprise was all about cinnamon” → C as rhetoric; cinnamon is central but not sufficient to explain later fiscal/legal state formation.
- “Kandy did not profit at all from international trade” → D if read literally; use only as a claim about VOC attempts to monopolize coastal access.
- “22 forts = complete control” → D if causalized directly; fort networks reduce transaction and coercion costs but resistance and inland trade continue.
- “slave quarters / slave labour” → B as panel evidence, to be cross-checked against VOC archival scholarship.
