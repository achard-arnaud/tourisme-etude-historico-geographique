# Run19 — Ishikawa fragments: coffee abandonment and tea conversion

Status: fragments only; **not claims**. These fragments must be sanitized and sourced before promotion.

## Material / biological
- Coffee rust persistence may have made Arabica replanting uneconomic in the wet zone.
- Wet-zone humidity and altitude may have favoured pathogen pressure.
- Narrow planting genetics may have increased vulnerability.

## Method / agronomy
- Monoculture and low varietal diversification may have amplified disease exposure.
- Large-scale fungicide control was not available at the relevant moment.
- Replanting implied a multi-year revenue gap into a known disease environment.

## Machine / capital
- Tea requires factory capital that coffee did not require to the same degree.
- Planter insolvency may have transferred estates toward agency-house and London joint-stock ownership.
- Continuous tea harvesting may have fitted leveraged corporate cash-flow requirements better than seasonal coffee.

## Manpower
- A resident imported workforce already existed before full tea conversion.
- Year-round tea plucking may have increased the value of retaining a permanent workforce.
- Gendered plucking organization is a candidate institutional persistence, not a timeless cultural fact.

## Milieu / geography
- Coffee-era road and rail infrastructure was a sunk asset inherited by successor crops.
- Elevation zoning may have created stronger quality differentiation for tea.
- Cinchona is a mandatory intermediate step to test against any smooth coffee→tea narrative.

## Market / measurement
- Metropolitan tea demand and imperial import-substitution policy may have reduced demand risk for conversion.
- Existing brokerage, auction and shipping institutions may have been repurposed rather than rebuilt.

## Promotion gate
No fragment above becomes a claim until the lag test separates rust timing, ownership transition and resident labour density, and until each promoted statement has a typed source-backed record.
