# Causal map — colonial transition

## Main trunk
Kandyan need for a maritime counterweight to Portugal
→ VOC alliance and capture of Portuguese coastal forts
→ VOC refusal to relinquish conquered coastal nodes
→ fort-chain + ports + monopoly trade + local intermediaries
→ increasingly territorial/fiscal colonial state
→ cadastral/legal/social classification and labour extraction
→ British seizure of Dutch possessions in 1795–96
→ selective inheritance and enlargement of Dutch administrative legibility
→ island-wide British integration after 1815/1833
→ constitutional state delivered at independence in 1948.

## Cross-cutting mechanisms
- **Coast/interior**: not a static ethnic divide but a changing logistics and sovereignty problem.
- **India-facing north**: Jaffna–Mannar–Palk Strait connects elephants, pearls, textiles, migrants, translators and religious networks to South India.
- **Labour continuum**: enslaved labour, debt bondage, caste service and rajakariya/corvée must not be collapsed into one category, but they overlap in colonial extraction.
- **Paper state**: thombos, courts, land/person registers and codified custom make populations more legible and therefore more taxable/governable.
- **Fort system**: a distributed operating system for ports, customs, patrols, rival-European deterrence and inland coercion, not twenty-two independent monuments.
