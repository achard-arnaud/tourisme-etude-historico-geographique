# Structuring chronological arcs

An ARC begins and ends when the governing optimum changes materially: political regime, resource system, spatial centre, security environment, trade architecture, legitimacy system, social-access architecture or external configuration.

Each arc records entry rupture, causal question, drivers, amplifiers, consequences, non-causes, persistent structures, exit rupture and bridge to the next arc. Avoid ruler-by-ruler periodization unless succession changes the system.

## Vertical HIL threads
A mechanism such as caste, language, education, irrigation, religion or migration may persist across many arcs. Give it a stable thread ID/concept and reactivate it only where its role changes. Do not create a new chronological arc merely because new evidence appears for a persistent theme.

## Comparator anchoring
A comparator has a **home arc** in the main case. Attach it where the alternative mechanism becomes analytically useful; keep enough comparator chronology to avoid anachronism without displacing the home-country spine.

## Side-story handoff
Every validated/promoted `side_story` has exactly one home `arc`; cross-arc material lists `related_arcs`. A side story is never a workaround for failing the rupture test: if the governing optimum truly changes, create/split an arc; if not, a `detour`, `callback`, `comparator`, `portrait` or other side story may carry the lateral material. This skill assigns the temporal home but does not promote the box.

## Rupture test
Before splitting ask: did incentives/capacity actually change, or only leadership, rhetoric or evidence availability? If the optimum remains stable, retain one arc and use a sub-section, vertical thread or side-story candidate.

## Output
One `ARC.md` per period plus vertical-thread IDs and temporal-home decisions that downstream `composing-side-stories` can reference.
