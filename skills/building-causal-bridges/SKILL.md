# Building causal bridges

Create a bridge only between established anchors when the missing relation changes the explanation. Ask one question, research the smallest mechanism needed, close as `A/B/C/D/U`, then integrate or discard. Prevent bridge proliferation: curiosity without causal necessity belongs in a side box or backlog.

## Comparative bridge gate
A comparison is not a bridge until the mechanism is normalized. For each case record:
- **starting conditions**: hierarchy, institutions, language regime, human capital and political coalition;
- **intervention**: law, reservation/quota, language rule, welfare programme, mobilization, war or market reform;
- **mechanism**: how opportunity, status, incentives or state access actually change;
- **outcome**: education, representation, employment, migration, violence, growth or social mixing;
- **confounders**: war, geography, initial income, external trade, demography, regime type and timing.

Prefer same-scale and overlapping-period comparisons. If the cases differ materially in timing or institutions, label the bridge `analogy` rather than causal evidence.

Never infer that policy X caused outcome Y merely because another place combined X with better outcomes. Comparative outcomes can identify a mechanism or counterfactual worth testing; they do not close it by themselves.

## Promotion rule
Promote a comparative bridge into the causal spine only if it changes the interpretation of the focal case and survives confounder review. Otherwise render it as `Petit détour`, `Mais aussi`, or a research backlog item.
