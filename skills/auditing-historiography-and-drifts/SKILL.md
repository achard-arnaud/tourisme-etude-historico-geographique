# Auditing historiography and drifts

Audit `semantic`, `temporal`, `scale`, `causal`, `source`, `survivorship`, `translation`, `teleological`, `comparator` and `metric` drift. Also test vertical bias (elite, monumental, written, state, victor) and horizontal bias (modern borders, fixed ethnicity, centre/periphery, coast/interior, colonial inevitability).

## Comparator audit
Before accepting “A succeeded where B failed”, test:
- selection bias in choosing the comparator;
- different institutional level (province/state/country);
- different exposure to war, market size, federal transfers or migration;
- post-treatment variables presented as starting conditions;
- incompatible indicators or time windows;
- survivorship and availability bias in success stories.

## Teleology vocabulary
Treat phrases such as “missed the boat”, “inevitable decline”, “traditional society”, “modern caste-free model”, “natural capital”, “apartheid” or “failed to globalize” as hypotheses requiring operational definitions. Replace them with observable mechanisms where possible.

## Source-position audit
Ask who benefits from the narrative: state, insurgency, colonial administration, development institution, tourism body, diaspora organization, political movement or successful-region literature. An official programme target is not an achieved outcome; a celebrated development model is not its own counterfactual.

Record each material correction next to the claim and in the drift ledger; do not hide it in an appendix.

## Output
One drift audit per arc, shaped by `templates/drift-audit.md` and stored in `07_drifts/*.md`, with each material correction cross-referenced to its claim.
