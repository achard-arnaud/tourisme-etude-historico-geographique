# Auditing historiography and drifts

Audit `semantic`, `temporal`, `scale`, `causal`, `source`, `survivorship`, `translation`, `teleological`, `comparator` and `metric` drift. Also test vertical bias (elite, monumental, written, state, victor) and horizontal bias (modern borders, fixed ethnicity, centre/periphery, coast/interior, colonial inevitability).

## Comparator audit
Before accepting “A succeeded where B failed”, test selection bias, institutional level, exposure to war/market/federal transfers/migration, post-treatment variables, indicator/time-window compatibility and survivorship bias.

## Teleology vocabulary
Treat phrases such as “missed the boat”, “inevitable decline”, “traditional society”, “modern caste-free model”, “natural capital”, “apartheid” or “failed to globalize” as hypotheses requiring operational definitions. Replace them with observable mechanisms where possible.

## Source-position audit
Ask who benefits from the narrative: state, insurgency, colonial administration, development institution, tourism body, diaspora organization, political movement or successful-region literature. A target is not an outcome; a celebrated model is not its own counterfactual.

## Side-story handoff
When a rejected interpretation is pedagogically useful, nominate `false_lead`; when the correction explains evidence handling, nominate `method`. The future `side_story` must reference the drift-audit path and underlying claim/source lineage. A correction cannot be promoted merely because it is rhetorically attractive, and a side story must preserve the rejection/uncertainty rather than resurrecting the drift.

Record each material correction next to the claim and in the drift ledger.

## Output
One drift audit per arc in `07_drifts/*.md`, with material corrections cross-referenced to claims and optional candidate handoff to `composing-side-stories`.
