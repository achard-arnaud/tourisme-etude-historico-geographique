# Analyzing institutions and power

Ask who can decide, tax, mobilize labour, enforce rules, appoint officials, control succession and claim legitimacy. Separate formal institutions from coalition practice. Track path dependence across conquest, restoration and regime change. Distinguish state capacity from territorial size.

## Access architecture
When the issue is inclusion/exclusion, map the rule that allocates scarce positions rather than using generic labels such as discrimination or meritocracy:
- language requirements;
- examinations and credential rules;
- caste/community reservations or reserved seats;
- district/regional quotas;
- citizenship and franchise;
- patronage, lineage or inherited office;
- school/university capacity and geographic access.

Record both the **distributional intent** and the **distributional effect**. A reform can democratize access for one excluded majority while simultaneously creating a new barrier for another minority.

## Representation caution
Do not infer political exclusion from minority status alone. Measure representation where possible and distinguish elected office, senior administration, lower civil service, professions, universities and security institutions. Perceived overrepresentation can be politically causal even when the underlying mechanism is colonial education rather than formal preference.
