# Analyzing society and demography

Separate documented migration from assumed population replacement. Track elites, soldiers, merchants, religious specialists, peasants, labourers and diasporas independently. Ask whose mobility changes institutions, production or military capacity. Treat ethnic and caste labels as historically variable unless continuity is demonstrated.

## Social-reproduction matrix
When hierarchy may matter, do not stop at naming groups. Trace six layers:
1. **status order** — caste, class, clan/kudi, religion, gender and locality;
2. **institutional encoding** — census, law, registers, service obligations, schools, temples, property and administrative categories;
3. **mobility channels** — education, state employment, professions, capital, land, migration and military service;
4. **reproduction channels** — endogamy, marriage negotiation, dowry/inheritance, neighbourhood, occupation, temple honours and kin networks;
5. **contestation** — anti-discrimination law, reservations/affirmative action, social movements, conversion, unions, insurgent regulation and litigation;
6. **transformation** — war, displacement, diaspora, urbanization, market integration and generational change.

Distinguish **public attenuation** of hierarchy from **private persistence**. A caste order can retreat from official politics while remaining consequential in marriage, property, temple governance or networks. Conversely, affirmative-action categories can make caste more visible administratively while reducing some material inequalities.

## Cleavage overlay
Test whether a larger conflict changes which social cleavage is politically salient. External ethnic, religious or state pressure can temporarily subordinate internal caste/class conflict without abolishing it. Record the mechanism explicitly rather than inferring social unity from nationalist rhetoric.

## Marriage and kinship caution
Never equate arranged marriage with forced marriage, endogamy with coercion, or one ethnographic case with a population rate. Treat marriage as a possible mechanism of property transfer, status reproduction, migration, alliance and diaspora reconstruction, and source each mechanism separately.

## Comparative use
For cross-country comparison, compare mechanisms rather than labels: who is excluded, what policy instrument redistributes opportunity, which institutions implement it, and which inequalities survive. Do not conclude that one society has 'more caste' without a stable comparable measure.
