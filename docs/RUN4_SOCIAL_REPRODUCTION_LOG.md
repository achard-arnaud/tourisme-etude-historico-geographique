# Run 4 — social reproduction / caste / comparative mobility

## Trigger
Jaffna fieldwork raised a missing social question: whether caste, marriage, education and access to the state should become a vertical causal layer in the Sri Lanka corpus, and whether Tamil Nadu / India / Indonesia offer useful mechanism-based comparators.

## Research decision
Caste is promoted to a **vertical social-reproduction axis**, not a master cause of civil war or economic divergence.

## Major corrections
- Ceylon Tamils were not simply underrepresented in the late-colonial state; English-educated Tamil elites often performed strongly in civil-service and professional channels.
- Sri Lanka did legislate against caste discrimination in 1957 and strengthened enforcement in 1971.
- colonial rule did not invent caste; registration/law could rework and harden hierarchy.
- Tamil Nadu did not abolish caste; its distinctive feature is the explicit use of reservations/social-justice politics to widen access.
- arranged marriage is not synonymous with forced marriage.
- LTTE-era nationalism did not erase caste authority.
- contemporary Sri Lanka is not legally an apartheid regime; the stronger claim is segmented multilingual institutions with uneven implementation and war-shaped territorial separation.
- Jaffna's human capital was partly externalized through migration/diaspora rather than simply destroyed.

## New bridges
1. Maviddapuram caste conflict -> defensive Tamil nationalism / cleavage overlay.
2. defensive nationalism -> partial suppression, not elimination, of caste under militancy.
3. Sri Lankan anti-discrimination architecture -> Tamil Nadu reservation/social-justice comparator.
4. Sinhala Only -> Bahasa Indonesia as bounded national-language comparator.
5. war/marriage/diaspora -> territorial loss + externalized human capital.
