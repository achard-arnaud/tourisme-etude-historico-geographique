## A14 — plastic pollution
- World Bank project documents describe Meethotamulla as a 14 April 2017 waste-landslide trigger for solid-waste policy, with 32 deaths, 8 missing, 79 homes destroyed and 264 families affected.
- UNEP's 2021 advisory mission calls X-Press Pearl Sri Lanka's worst maritime disaster and its plastic spill the largest on record.
- A peer-reviewed ACS paper estimates roughly 1,680 tonnes of nurdles spilled.
- Sri Lanka Fisheries Ministry reporting identifies 15,032 directly employed fishers and 4,888 related workers for livelihood compensation.
- Cabinet records establish successive single-use-plastic restrictions, but not enforcement success.
- UK Environment Agency reporting establishes one discrete 21-container illegal waste-shipment case returned from Sri Lanka in 2020.

**Bounded conclusion:** domestic waste failure, maritime accident and transboundary illegal waste are distinct mechanisms. The evidence here refutes one-source attribution but does not estimate their long-run relative shares.

## A15 — tsunami
- Joint-needs-assessment / World Bank / ADB material anchors a toll above 31,000 deaths and roughly 440,000 displaced, with about 60% of damage concentrated on east/northeast coasts.
- Recovery analysis documents the 100/200m setback policy, relocation and rapid revision/abandonment of the original rule during 2005.
- Refworld/Freedom House and HRW syntheses document the P-TOMS aid-sharing intent, JVP opposition/coalition withdrawal and judicial blockage.

**Bounded conclusion:** the durable-reconciliation narrative fails. The stronger chain from P-TOMS failure to the 2006 return to war remains unresolved because multiple conflict dynamics are sufficient confounders.

## A16 — tourism and fiscal collapse
- SLTDA records 2,333,796 arrivals in 2018, 1,913,702 in 2019 (-18%), 507,704 in 2020, and 194,495 in 2021.
- IMF and World Bank analyses separately document the 2019 tax cuts/revenue collapse, fertilizer/import restrictions and commercial-debt vulnerability.
- CBSL documents remittance-channel stress and the mid-April 2022 suspension of selected external debt-service payments.

**Bounded conclusion:** the tourism shock was large and persistent but is not sufficient as a monocausal explanation of default. The 2019 Easter shock is a useful control case, but not a perfectly comparable natural experiment.
