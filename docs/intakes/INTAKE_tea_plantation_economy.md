A large, densely interlinked subject. This package untangles it into four
analytical passes, then converts each pass into arcs, fragments, bridges and
side stories. Work the passes in order: the value-capture question cannot be
answered before the conversion question, because the modern structure is the
colonial structure with different owners.

## 1. Tree of thought — the rival explanations to test

The whole subject fails if the corpus adopts one explanation before testing the
others. Three for the conversion, three for the modern value split. They are
not mutually exclusive; the test is **which explains the timing and which
explains the form**.

### 1.1 Why tea replaced coffee — three hypotheses

**H1 — Biological determinism.** *Hemileia vastatrix* made Arabica
unrepeatable in the wet zone; tea was immune; there was no choice.
*Predicts:* conversion timing tracks the rust front, estate by estate.
*Weakness:* it does not explain why the intermediate crop, cinchona, was
abandoned, nor why the wet-zone lowlands went to rubber rather than tea.

**H2 — Capital-form determinism.** Rust destroyed planter-proprietors, and the
capital that replaced them was London joint-stock. Tea requires an on-estate
factory and continuous working capital; it therefore *fitted the new capital
form*. The crop was selected by the financing structure, not the fungus.
*Predicts:* conversion timing tracks ownership change and agency-house
consolidation, lagging the rust front by years.
*This is the strongest and least tested hypothesis. Prioritise it.*

**H3 — Labour-system conservation.** The binding asset was not land but an
immobile, indebted workforce assembled over forty years. Coffee's seasonal
harvest could not hold it; tea's year-round plucking could. The crop chosen was
the one that preserved the labour system.
*Predicts:* estates with the largest resident labour forces convert fastest,
regardless of rust arrival date or ownership.

**Test that discriminates:** the lag structure. If conversion follows the rust
front → H1. If it follows ownership transfer → H2. If it follows resident
labour density → H3. District-level data on estate ownership, acreage under
each crop by year, and resident population exists in colonial administration
reports. This is a real, resolvable question.

### 1.2 Why so little retail value stays on the island — three hypotheses

**M1 — Structural/commodity.** Auction-sold bulk commodity; branding, blending
and retail happen in consuming countries; the producer is structurally a
price-taker.
*Weakness:* Sri Lanka exports an unusually high share in packaged form
compared with other origins. If M1 were sufficient, that share would not exist.

**M2 — Productive.** Yield stagnation, ageing bushes, near-absent replanting,
fragmentation into smallholdings, and cost per kilo above competitors. The
island loses value because it produces expensively, not because it sells badly.

**M3 — Rent allocation.** Capital available to the sector is deliberately
redeployed away from agronomy — into hospitality, real estate, branding,
diversified crops — because those returns beat replanting a bush that yields in
five years on a leasehold. Underinvestment is a rational choice, not a failure.
*This is the user hypothesis, and there is now a live 2026 mechanism that
supports it — see §5.4. Do not treat that as confirmation; treat it as the
first testable instance.*

**Test that discriminates:** compare capital expenditure allocation inside the
regional plantation companies across a decade — replanting and mechanisation
versus non-agricultural segments — against their own reported segment returns.
Company annual reports are public.

## 2. Pass 1 — Ishikawa: why coffee was not replanted

Effect: **abandonment of coffee in Ceylon after the 1870s–1890s collapse.**

**Material / biological**
- Obligate biotrophic rust persists regionally; no resistant Arabica available
  at the time.
- Wet-zone humidity and the altitude band are optimal for the pathogen.
- Existing plantings were a genetically narrow Arabica population.

**Method / agronomy**
- Monoculture without shade or rotation; no varietal diversification.
- No fungicide chemistry existed at the scale required.
- Replanting means a 3–5 year revenue gap into a known pathogen reservoir —
  an unfinanceable proposition after a solvency shock.

**Machine / capital**
- Coffee needs only pulping and drying; tea needs a factory. Once the factory
  is built, its sunk cost locks the crop for decades.
- Bankruptcy of individual planters transferred estates to agency houses and
  London-registered companies with different hurdle rates.
- Tea's continuous harvest gives continuous cash flow — a decisive advantage
  for leveraged corporate ownership.

**Manpower**
- Resident indentured workforce already in place, immobile, indebted.
- Tea plucking is year-round and skill-intensive in a way that entrenches a
  permanent resident workforce; coffee's harvest peak would have released it.
- Gendered task allocation in plucking emerges here and persists to today.

**Milieu / geography**
- The rail line to Kandy and the hill roads were already built *for coffee* —
  sunk infrastructure that any successor crop inherits free.
- Elevation zoning suits tea quality differentiation in a way coffee did not
  reward.
- Cinchona as the intermediate crop, then its price collapse when supply moved
  elsewhere — the forgotten step, and a strong `false_lead` candidate against
  any smooth "coffee → tea" narrative.

**Market / measurement**
- Metropolitan demand for tea was already secured by tax and habit (§4).
- Import substitution against Chinese supply was an explicit imperial
  objective.
- Brokerage, auction and shipping institutions could be repointed at the new
  crop at near-zero cost.

**Fragment output:** each branch yields 2–4 `claim` or `inference` fragments.
Do not let the biological branch absorb the others: it is the most-told and the
least explanatory.

## 3. Pass 2 — Ishikawa: why value is captured downstream

Effect: **a small share of final retail value retained at origin.**

**Product**
- Bulk leaf is undifferentiated at the auction; grade is not brand.
- No protected geographical indication for tea comparable to what Ceylon
  cinnamon has obtained; the Lion mark is a certification mark, not an
  appellation. **Verify this precisely — it is load-bearing for §7.**
- Blending destroys origin: a blend is legally and commercially the blender's
  product.

**Process**
- Green leaf must reach a factory within hours of plucking. Perishability plus
  transport cost equals a **spatial monopsony** for every smallholder.
- Factory ownership is concentrated relative to leaf supply.
- Energy for withering and firing is a large, volatile cost.

**Place / market structure**
- Auction is a price-discovery mechanism, not a value-capture mechanism.
- Consuming-market brands own the shelf, the recipe and the consumer
  relationship.
- Tariff escalation in consuming markets historically taxed processed product
  above raw — check current schedules before asserting.

**People**
- Wage set administratively rather than by productivity bargaining.
- Attendance-conditional pay design.
- Skill and supervision structures inherited unchanged from the colonial
  period.

**Policy**
- Export levies and cess.
- Fertiliser and input policy — including the 2021 shock (§5.3).
- 53-year leasehold: the tenure horizon versus the replanting payback horizon
  is the single sharpest instrument question in the modern arc.

**Capital**
- Company capital allocation across segments (M3).
- Cost and availability of long-term agricultural credit.
- Smallholder access to any capital at all.

## 4. Pass 3 — Porter decomposition of the chain

Walk each stage and record three things: who owns it, what share of final value
it plausibly takes, and whether entry is contestable. Numbers are **targets to
source**, not assertions.

| Stage | Actor | Chokepoint | Where the rent sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land and bush | RPC leaseholds; smallholders (now the majority of production — verify share) | Tenure horizon vs replanting payback | Low; capital-starved |
| Green leaf | Estate labour; smallholder households | **Perishability window → spatial monopsony** | Lowest in the chain |
| Factory | RPC factories; bought-leaf factories | Capital threshold; energy | Moderate; price formula caps it |
| Auction / broking | Colombo auction, broking houses | Information and access | Small but secure margin |
| Export / packing | Sri Lankan packers — the island's real value-added success | Certification, buyer relationships | The one stage where origin captures more than commodity share |
| Blending | Consuming-market blenders | Recipe, scale, supply flexibility | Substantial |
| Brand and retail | Global brands, retailers | Shelf, consumer relationship, advertising | Largest |

**Support activities to trace separately:** research and extension (the tea
research institute), input supply, transport, certification bodies,
finance, and the tourism/experience layer now grafted onto estates.

**The single most important observation to test:** the perishability window at
stage two is not a market imperfection, it is a physical constraint that
*creates* monopsony wherever factory density is low. That makes the monopsony
geographic, and therefore mappable against relief. See §5.

## 5. Pass 4 — the crossings the user asked for

### 5.1 Monopsony × relief × rurality

Three superimposed monopsonies, and they are not the same object:

1. **Labour monopsony on the estate.** One employer, employer-provided housing,
   employer-provided crèche and dispensary, and a valley with no alternative
   employer within commuting distance on foot. Exit requires migration, and
   migration requires documents, capital and a receiving community.
2. **Leaf monopsony over smallholders.** Set by the perishability window and
   road distance. Mappable: draw the catchment of each factory against the
   terrain and the monopsony appears as a map, not as a metaphor.
3. **Buyer monopsony/oligopsony at the top of the chain**, over the origin as a
   whole.

The relief is the mechanism that converts all three from theory into
enforceable fact. Steep, dissected valleys with single access roads mean the
labour market is not national — it is per-valley.

**This is the finding to pursue.** It also explains why national labour-market
reasoning fails on the estate sector, and why the official statistics keep the
"estate sector" as a category distinct from urban and rural.

### 5.2 Housing — correcting the field observation

The housing model does exist and is highly visible once identified: the
**line room** (*layam* in Tamil), a contiguous single-storey barrack row of
small rooms, built from the nineteenth century and still housing a large share
of estate families. It is not detached and not dispersed, which is why it does
not read as "housing" from a valley road — it reads as an outbuilding.

Three consequences to research:
- Housing tied to employment converts a labour contract into a tenancy: losing
  the job means losing the dwelling. That is the mechanism by which monopsony
  becomes durable across generations.
- Land grant policy — repeated commitments to allocate small individual plots
  to estate families — is the instrument intended to break precisely that link.
  Trace commitments against completions. Expect a wide gap.
- **Siting.** Line rooms occupy the land the estate did not want to plant,
  which is disproportionately unstable slope. Cross this with the national
  landslide risk zoning and with specific slope failures that killed estate
  workers in their lines. This is a direct bridge from housing form to disaster
  mortality, and it is the strongest single argument in the arc.

### 5.3 The 2021 shock — correcting the premise

The user's framing ("post-Covid, the president restricts imports to protect the
commercial banking system, forcing an upmarket move") needs splitting into two
separate objects:

- `policy_intent`: the agrochemical import ban of April 2021 was announced as a
  transition to organic agriculture.
- The **fiscal and foreign-exchange motive**: import compression to defend
  reserves. Announced intent and operative motive are different records, and
  both are evidenced.
- `policy_effect`: yield collapse across tea, and disproportionate impact on
  smallholders with no buffer. Verify magnitude and duration.
- **Second-order effect, and the non-obvious finding:** a forced, unprepared
  "organic" transition discredited the organic category domestically. That
  matters for §7 — it means the organic pathway is not merely economically
  marginal, it is politically contaminated.

There was no "forced move upmarket". There was a yield shock followed by a
partial reversal. Record the difference.

### 5.4 Capital allocation — the live 2026 mechanism

In January 2026 the daily estate wage was raised to 1,750 rupees, composed of a
1,550 basic plus a 200 attendance allowance, with the increase split between
plantation companies and the state, and a budget line of 5,000 million rupees
funding the public share.

Three things to record, carefully and separately:

1. `source_fact` — the wage, the composition, the split, the date, the amount.
2. `policy_effect` (to test) — an attendance-conditional design can reduce
   effective earnings for workers who cannot achieve full attendance, and
   coverage is reported as partial relative to the estate workforce. Verify
   both.
3. `inference` (to test, and the important one) — **a public subsidy of the
   wage bill removes pressure on companies to raise productivity.** If the
   state closes the wage gap, the return to mechanisation and replanting falls
   rather than rises. That is the user's hypothesis with an identified,
   dated, documented mechanism.

**Do not assert (3).** It is the analytical core of the arc and it must be
built as a bridge with confounders: leasehold horizon, terrain limiting
mechanisation (plucking on steep slopes resists machines for quality reasons
too), union positions on mechanisation, and the fact that the subsidy is recent
while underinvestment is decades old — the timing does not fit a simple causal
story, and that mismatch is itself a finding.

### 5.5 Unions — correcting the premise

There is no absence of agricultural unions. Estate unions are among the oldest
and most organised in the country, and their leaderships have simultaneously
been political parties and coalition partners for decades. The analytical
object is therefore not absence but **dual role**: a union that is also an
ethnic political broker and a government partner negotiates differently from
one that is only a union.

Research targets: the biennial collective agreement mechanism; the statutory
wages board and whether the 2026 settlement bypassed it; the relationship
between union membership, electoral mobilisation and ministerial posts.
Treat competing readings as positions to record, not to arbitrate.

## 7. Why fair trade, organic and appellation may not be development levers

The user's intuition deserves a proper test rather than agreement. Build it as
a bridge with a real chance of failing.

**Arguments for the negative (to evidence, not to assume):**
- Certification has fixed costs per unit audited, which fall hardest on
  fragmented smallholders — precisely the segment now producing the majority.
- A premium captured by a certification body or a buyer's programme is not a
  premium captured by a household. Trace the premium's path, not its existence.
- An appellation transfers rent to the *collective holder of the name*. If the
  name is already owned as a certification mark administered by a state board,
  the marginal gain from a geographical indication is institutional, not
  commercial. Verify what protection actually exists and where.
- Organic carries a yield penalty that a low-yield, ageing-bush sector can
  least afford — and after 2021 the category is domestically discredited.

**Arguments for the positive (to test with equal seriousness):**
- Ceylon cinnamon obtained European protected status; the institutional pathway
  therefore exists and has been walked successfully by another Sri Lankan crop.
  Why not tea? That comparison is internal, so the "different state" confounder
  disappears.
- Single-estate and single-region marketing already commands price premia in
  specialty channels.
- The island's high share of value-added export is evidence that moving
  downstream is possible, not that it is blocked.

**The analogy the user proposed needs correcting.** Lipton and Twinings are not
Champagne houses. Champagne houses sit *inside* the appellation and their
interest is aligned with defending it; the great tea brands sit *outside* the
origin entirely and their interest is in blend flexibility, which is the
opposite of origin protection. The right comparisons are the négociant model in
wine, and Swiss chocolate — a world-famous processing brand built on a raw
material grown elsewhere by people who capture very little. Use chocolate as
the comparator, not Champagne: the structure matches.

## 10. Premise corrections to carry into the research

Five, all of which change what gets researched:

1. **Line rooms exist** — the housing model is present, contiguous and barrack

2. **Unions exist** and are old, large and politically embedded. The object is

3. **Lipton and Twinings are not Champagne houses** — they are outside the

4. **2021 was not a forced move upmarket** — it was an import ban with an

5. **China is not the principal export competitor.** Chinese volume is very
   large but heavily domestically consumed and largely green. The competitive
   pressure on black-tea export markets comes from elsewhere — verify which
   origin, and on what segment, before writing anything about competition.

## 12. Manufacture — the six steps, and why they matter causally

Not decoration. Each step is a cost centre, a quality lever and a point where
the chain could have been reorganised and was not.

1. **Plucking.** Two leaves and a bud is a quality standard, not a botanical
   fact; coarser plucking raises weight and lowers grade. The plucking standard
   is therefore the point where a piece-rate wage system and a quality system
   collide directly. Every wage negotiation is also a quality negotiation.
2. **Withering.** Twelve to eighteen hours on troughs, moisture down by roughly
   half. Physical wither (limpness for rolling) and chemical wither (precursor
   formation) are distinct processes in the same operation. Energy-hungry, and
   the reason factories sit on the estate rather than in town.
3. **Rolling.** Orthodox rollers twist and rupture the leaf cells, releasing
   enzyme and substrate. **Sri Lanka is overwhelmingly orthodox rather than
   CTC** — verify the current share. That is a strategic commitment: orthodox
   preserves whole-leaf grades and higher unit price; CTC maximises volume and
   extraction for teabags. The island chose the price segment and thereby chose
   its yield ceiling.
4. **Oxidation.** Enzymatic, driven by polyphenol oxidase, 60–120 minutes under
   controlled temperature and humidity. The trade calls it "fermentation",
   which is a **misnomer** — no microbial fermentation occurs. Worth one
   `method` note: an industry that misnames its own core process by two hundred
   years of habit is a small but exact illustration of how vocabulary is
   inherited rather than examined.
5. **Firing / drying.** Hot air halts the enzymes and takes moisture to around
   three per cent. Fuel cost, and historically a driver of local deforestation
   for firewood — a direct environmental link that is usually missed because it
   is inside the factory, not on the slope.
6. **Sorting, grading, packing.** Mesh sizes separate particle sizes; fibre is
   extracted; lots are made up for auction.

Green tea inserts a fixation step (steam or pan) that denatures the enzyme
before rolling, so no oxidation occurs. White tea is wither and dry only. Same
plant, different process — which is the fact that answers "does tea come from
China" more usefully than any origin story.

## 13. The grading system is a buyer's vocabulary

This is the section that changes the argument in §7, so treat it carefully.

**British-derived leaf grades — OP, OPA, FOP, FBOP, BOP, BOPF, Fannings, Dust,
Pekoe, Souchong — classify particle size and leaf appearance. They do not
classify taste, and they do not classify origin.** Verify the exact grade set
in current use against the tea board's own nomenclature before writing.

Three consequences:

- **The fine grades are teabag feedstock.** Fannings and dust extract fast and
  give colour quickly, which is what a bag in a mug requires. The classification
  therefore encodes the needs of downstream blending and bagging. The producer
  describes its own product in the buyer's categories.
- **No grade names a terroir.** Unlike wine, where the classification is
  geographic and the vocabulary belongs to the producing region, the tea grade
  vocabulary is functional and belongs to the trade. Sri Lanka's own geographic
  distinctions — the elevation bands, and the regional characters within
  them — sit *outside* the grading system and have to be marketed separately.
- Therefore the weakness of terroir against brand is not only commercial. **The
  language in which the product is sold has no slot for origin.** That is a
  structural finding, and it is the strongest single argument for why an
  appellation strategy faces a harder problem here than in wine.

Also worth one `method` note: "pekoe" derives from a Chinese term for the white
down on the bud, and the "orange" in orange pekoe is conventionally traced to
the Dutch house of Orange, though the etymology is contested and has nothing to
do with the fruit or with flavour. A grade name that most consumers misread as
a taste descriptor is a small, memorable illustration of the whole section.

## 14. Lipton — the pitch, and the reversal

**1890.** A Glasgow grocer with a chain of shops buys Ceylon estates outright,
at the bottom of the market left by the coffee collapse. Dambatenne and its
neighbours near Haputale. The slogan is the strategy: direct from the tea
garden to the teapot.

**The innovation is not the tea.** It is the elimination of the intermediate
chain and the invention of the branded fixed-weight packet at a fixed price,
sold to households that previously bought loose tea of unverifiable quality
from a grocer's chest. Vertical integration from bush to shelf, plus
packaging, plus price transparency, plus advertising. He owned the origin, the
processing, the brand and the retail.

**The reversal is the pitch.** Follow the ownership of the two assets — the
land and the name — across a century:

| | Estate | Brand |
|---|---|---|
| 1890 | British owner | Same British owner |
| 1948 | Largely British corporate | British corporate |
| 1975 | Nationalised — Sri Lankan state | British / Anglo-Dutch multinational |
| 1992–95 | Privatised — Sri Lankan companies | Multinational |
| 2022 onward | Sri Lankan companies | European private equity |

**Land ownership was decolonised. Name ownership was not.** The most valuable
asset created on Ceylonese soil left the island and never returned, and it left
by sale rather than by seizure, which is why no political process ever
addressed it.

Two things to research rather than assert:
- The current corporate ownership chain of the brand, precisely and with dates,
  including the divestment of the tea business by its previous multinational
  owner and the private-equity acquisition that followed.
- **The tourism inversion.** The viewpoint above the original estate is now
  marketed under the brand's name as a visitor attraction. The origin story is
  monetised locally as an experience while the brand rent accrues elsewhere.
  That is the "experiential capitalism" hypothesis in a single site, and it is
  photographable — a strong `object_focus` or `portrait` candidate.

## 15. The béké parallel — challenged, then repaired

**As proposed, the parallel fails**, and the way it fails is the useful part.

The Antillean planter class was **resident and creole**: it stayed through
abolition, was compensated at abolition — the indemnity went to owners, not to
the freed — and converted plantation capital into import and distribution
positions that it still holds. Departmentalisation rather than independence
meant there was never a sovereign moment at which expropriation was politically
available.

Ceylon differs on every one of those:

| | Antilles | Ceylon |
|---|---|---|
| Planter class | Resident, creole, multi-generational | Largely **absentee**, corporate, London-registered |
| Political trajectory | Departmentalisation, no independence | Independence 1948 |
| Post-colonial fate of estates | Retained, converted | **Expropriated** — land reform 1972, nationalisation 1975 |
| Successor owners | Same families | Different class: domestic conglomerates, from 1992–95 |
| Compensation | Indemnity to owners at abolition | Compensation terms of the 1970s nationalisation — verify |

Absentee ownership is what made nationalisation politically cheap in the 1970s:
there was no resident oligarchy embedded in the national community to defend.
That is a real causal difference, and it deserves to be the finding rather than
the analogy being forced.

**The repaired comparison — and the better one — is internal.** The class that
actually performed a béké-like conversion in Ceylon is the **low-country
Sinhalese commercial elite**: families that accumulated in nineteenth-century
arrack renting, graphite, coconut and coastal trade, bought into estates and
urban property, and converted commercial rent into political and industrial
position after independence. That is a resident class converting colonial-era
rents into post-colonial power — the mechanism the béké comparison was reaching
for, available domestically, with caste as the legible marker.

This connects directly to the caste threads already in the corpus and removes
the "different state" confounder entirely.

**Bridge specification:**
- `comparative: true`, `home_claim`: Ceylon capital transition at independence;
  `comparator_claim`: Antillean planter conversion.
- `transportability`: **`mechanism`** — conversion of plantation rent into
  distribution and political position. Never `outcome`.
- `confounders[]`: resident versus absentee ownership; independence versus
  departmentalisation; presence or absence of an indemnity; metropolitan
  citizenship; timing of abolition relative to the crop cycle; racial
  composition of the owning class.
- `bounded_by`: the comparison shows an alternative institutional route was
  available, not that Ceylon would have followed it.
