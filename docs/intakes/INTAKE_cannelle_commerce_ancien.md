# Intake : Cannelle et commerce ancien (routes musulmanes, Océan Indien) — Pourquoi le marché existe mais ne se massifie pas

**Période : VIII–XV e s. (avant intrusion portugaise)**

## Preservation note
Original analytical intake supplied by the user on 2026-09-02. It is preserved as a research trigger, not as evidence. Run41 qualifies its propositions against independent sources before promotion.

## Core propositions preserved

- The cinnamon/spice trade through the Indian Ocean predates Portuguese intervention by centuries.
- Muslim, Indian and Chinese actors possessed extensive route knowledge; the route was not a European geographical discovery.
- Sri Lanka was connected to Indian Ocean exchange through coastal merchants and ports while inland political economy remained largely agrarian/hydraulic.
- Zheng He’s voyages demonstrate high-level Ming knowledge and projection into Sri Lankan waters before 1500.
- The intake hypothesizes a low-volume/high-margin prestige economy before Portuguese intervention and attributes limited massification to transport costs, demand structure and intermediary incentives.

## Qualification flags

The supplied numeric estimates for annual volumes, losses in transit, price multipliers and potential production are illustrative and are not promoted without archival or specialist quantitative anchors. The claim that Muslim intermediaries deliberately maintained scarcity is retained as a hypothesis, not a fact. The statement that China simply “chose not to dominate” is bounded: Ming withdrawal was political, but maritime policy, fiscal priorities and institutional constraints are more complex than a single isolationist decision.
