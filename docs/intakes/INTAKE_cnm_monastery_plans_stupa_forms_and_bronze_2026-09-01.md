## A. Monastery Plans panel
The panel presents three principal architectural types for Buddhist monasteries in the Anuradhapura period.

### Mahavihara / city monasteries
The panel groups Mahavihara with Ruwanwelisaya, Abhayagiri Vihara and Jetavanarama as large urban monastic complexes organized around a central colossal stupa with courts and monks' residences. It also mentions Bodhi-tree shrines, image houses and chapter houses such as Lohapasada and Ratnapasada. The panel compares the outward stepped appearance of multi-storeyed chapter houses to a stepped pyramid / ziggurat; this comparison is retained as museum analogy only, not as a historical derivation claim.

### Tapovana monasteries
The panel describes double-platform monasteries with two terraces joined by a stone gangway, simple architecture with little decoration, and a meditative monastic lifestyle. Its claim that this lifestyle explains the absence of separate stupas and image houses is treated as an institutional interpretation requiring corroboration rather than as a closed causal fact.

### Pabbatavihara / moated monasteries
The panel describes terraced, moat-surrounded monasteries, with sacred structures on the upper terrace and monks' cells on the lower terrace. It states that copper plaques bearing quotations from Mahayana texts found enshrined within stupas indicate Mahayanistic influence. This is a high-value lead for A02c/HIL-05 and should be independently anchored before strong promotion.
