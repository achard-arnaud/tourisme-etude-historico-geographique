## Museum-panel propositions preserved as leads

### Political sequence
- Dambadeniya (1232–1271): retreat/relocation after Polonnaruwa, resistance to Magha, Parākramabāhu II as major ruler.
- Chandrabhānu of Tambralinga/Malay Peninsula invades Lanka in the mid-thirteenth century and is defeated; a second invasion follows.
- Yapahuwa (1272–c.1300): Bhuvanekabāhu I strengthens a fortress capital; Arab merchants act as intermediaries on the western coast; in 1283 an embassy is sent to the Mamluk Sultan of Egypt seeking direct trade without intermediaries; an Arya Cakravarti connected with the Pāṇḍyas invades and removes the Tooth and Bowl relics.
- Kurunegala (c.1300–1326) succeeds Yapahuwa.
- Gampola (fourteenth–early fifteenth centuries): Arya Cakravarti power extends southward; Nissanka Alagakkonara fortifies Kotte; Chinese intervention under Zheng He culminates in the capture/removal of Alakeshvara in 1411.
- Kotte (fifteenth–early sixteenth centuries): Parākramabāhu VI strengthens the kingdom, defeats/absorbs Jaffna for a period through Prince Sapumal, while later fragmentation enables Jaffna to re-establish independence; Portuguese intervention begins in the early sixteenth century.
- Sitawaka (sixteenth century): inland counterweight to Portuguese-supported Kotte; Kandy remains independent.

### Economy and society
- The shift of political centres toward the wetter south-west is associated by the museum with reduced dependence on the old large dry-zone hydraulic surplus and greater weight of rain-fed/export crops, coconut/arecanut/tamarind, spices, gems and trade.
- Arab and Muslim merchants appear as important intermediaries in long-distance trade.
- Monetisation and taxation continue/develop through the transitional period.
- South Indian trading and artisan groups are absorbed into local social structures.

### Religion and culture
- The Tooth Relic continues as a portable palladium of kingship: as capitals move, the relic/shrine moves with them.
- The museum frames repeated monastic “cleansing”/disciplinary reforms as responses to perceived deterioration of the Saṅgha; this wording must be treated as historiographically loaded and independently sourced.
- Hindu/Buddhist synthesis and Brahmanical influence are visible in Gampola/Kotte religious patronage.
- Muslim communities settle in trading towns and Islamic institutions grow on the coast.
- Literary and inscriptional production continues in Sinhala, Tamil and Sanskrit.

## Narrative decomposition — candidate threads

### T1 — The kingdom that learned to move
**Scene:** Polonnaruwa is lost as durable capital after the thirteenth-century rupture.
**Mechanism:** sovereignty becomes portable: court, relic, defensive site and fiscal network can be reassembled in a new place.
**Sequence:** Dambadeniya → Yapahuwa → Kurunegala → Gampola/Raigama → Kotte.
**Return:** Kandy later inherits an already-established logic of defensible inland sovereignty rather than inventing it from nothing.

### T2 — The Tooth Relic as portable state infrastructure
**Scene:** Arya Cakravarti removes the Tooth Relic from Yapahuwa.
**Mechanism:** possession of the relic is not only devotional; it anchors claims to legitimate kingship.
**Narrative use:** track the relic’s movement together with capital relocation to show that legitimacy itself becomes spatially mobile.

### T3 — An island invaded from more than one direction
**Scene:** Chandrabhānu arrives from Tambralinga, not India.
**Mechanism:** Sri Lanka lies inside Bay of Bengal as well as South Indian military/political systems.
**Bridge:** this breaks a simplistic “Sri Lanka versus South India” frame and reconnects Polonnaruwa/Dambadeniya to Southeast Asia.

### T4 — Pandyan power reaches Lanka through an Arya Cakravarti
**Scene:** Yapahuwa is penetrated and the relic is taken.
**Mechanism:** the Palk Strait is a political corridor; Pandyan imperial reach can operate through commanders/chieftains whose descendants become central to northern Sri Lankan politics.
**Bridge:** transition from Pandyan intervention to the rise/consolidation of Jaffna.

### T5 — The Muslim merchant becomes diplomat
**Scene:** in 1283 Bhuvanekabāhu I sends a mission to the Mamluk court of Sultan Qalāwūn, led by the Muslim envoy al-Hajj Abū ʿUthmān.
**Mechanism:** Muslim merchant networks are not peripheral minorities but carriers of commercial intelligence, language and diplomatic connectivity.
**Bridge:** Mannar/pearls/gems/spices → western Indian Ocean → Aden/Egypt.

### T6 — China does not merely trade; it intervenes
**Scene:** Zheng He’s forces defeat/capture Alakeshvara during the Ming–Kotte conflict (1410–1411 chronology varies in scholarship).
**Mechanism:** control of Sri Lanka’s ports and maritime routes matters enough for Ming power to intervene militarily and reorder local politics.
**Bridge:** ancient Chinese pilgrims/traders → Ming maritime state power → later Portuguese armed trade.

### T7 — Kotte turns toward the ports
**Scene:** the fortified capital is inland, but its political economy is tied to Colombo/Beruwala and coastal customs.
**Mechanism:** after the dry-zone hydraulic order contracts, rulers increasingly rely on port taxes, export commodities and control of coastal gateways.
**Bridge:** this is not “collapse into small kingdoms” only; it is also adaptation to a different revenue geography.

### T8 — Jaffna as both rival and interface
**Scene:** Kotte conquers Jaffna under Sapumal; Jaffna later recovers autonomy.
**Mechanism:** northern sovereignty is embedded in South Indian military, religious and commercial networks while still participating in island-wide rivalry.
**Bridge:** Jaffna/Mannar/perles/chevaux ↔ Vijayanagara/Pāṇḍya worlds ↔ Kotte.

## Epistemic guardrails
- Do not treat “Transitional Period” as a neutral natural periodisation; it is a retrospective historiographic label organised around Polonnaruwa/Kandy.
- Do not describe every capital change as flight. Different moves combine defence, succession, sacred legitimacy, wet-zone agrarian adaptation and access to trade revenues.
- Do not conflate Arya Cakravarti as Pandyan commander in the 1280s with a fully formed later Jaffna kingdom without tracing the institutional transition.
- Do not portray Arab/Muslim merchants as an undifferentiated monopoly; use them as documented intermediaries within multi-ethnic networks.
- Do not reproduce the museum’s “decline in morality of the Sangha” as an objective fact without identifying the normative source tradition.
