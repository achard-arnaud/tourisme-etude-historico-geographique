# WORKSTREAM A — Caste, status, mobility and the India/Sri Lanka comparison

## 1. Existing corpus fit — extend, do not duplicate

The current post-1948 corpus already contains a social-reproduction vertical roughly structured as:

`caste/status -> education/English -> access to state/professions -> anti-caste law / quotas -> defensive nationalism -> war -> marriage/diaspora -> post-war mobility`

and already uses Tamil Nadu as a **mechanism comparator**, especially for reservations/social-justice instruments.

Run20 should therefore **challenge and deepen the existing arc**, not create a generic “caste in India” arc. The useful question is why superficially related Tamil-speaking societies generated different political technologies for hierarchy and mobility.

### Arc routing

Primary target: existing `A12_caste_social_mobility_comparative` (canonical ID must be re-read before edits).  
Cross-links: education/English, colonial administration, temples/religion, land, labour, nationalism, war/displacement, diaspora/marriage.

## 2. Mandatory conceptual split

Do not use the following terms as synonyms:

- `varna`: pan-Indian normative/classificatory ideology;
- `jati`: localized/endogamous social groups and occupational/status communities;
- `Dalit`: modern political/social identity widely used in India but **not automatically the local self-designation in Sri Lanka**;
- `Scheduled Caste`: Indian constitutional/administrative category;
- `Panchamar`: Sri Lankan Tamil/Jaffna grouping used in scholarship for several historically oppressed castes; usage and self-identification require care;
- `Vellalar`, `Karaiyar`, `Nalavar`, `Pallar`, `Paraiyar`, `Vannar`, `Ampattar`, etc.: locally situated caste names whose status, occupation and political role vary through time and place.

A major Run20 drift test is therefore:

> **Does importing the Indian `varna/Dalit/reservation` vocabulary into Jaffna obscure the local structure more than it clarifies it?**

## 3. Research questions to preregister

### C20-A1 — Structure
How did caste hierarchy in Jaffna differ structurally from simplified four-varna textbook models of India?

Tests:
- identify whether local hierarchy is better explained by land control, hereditary service relations, temple access, occupational specialization and endogamy than by direct varna mapping;
- distinguish ritual superiority from economic/political dominance;
- test whether Vellalar dominance depended on demographic weight, landholding, colonial office/education, temple patronage, or all four.

### C20-A2 — Law versus social practice
Sri Lanka prohibited caste-based social disabilities early, including via the Prevention of Social Disabilities Act No. 21 of 1957; later constitutional equality provisions prohibit discrimination including caste. Why did caste effects persist despite this legal architecture?

Mechanisms to test:
- weak enforcement / private discrimination;
- endogamy and marriage markets;
- temple governance and religious institutions;
- land/housing and inherited wealth;
- education and occupational networks;
- concealment rather than disappearance of caste identity.

### C20-A3 — Tamil Nadu divergence
Why did Tamil Nadu institutionalize a large, explicit reservation/social-justice architecture while Sri Lanka did not build an equivalent caste-coded redistribution system?

Rival explanations:
- `H-A3.1 political_mobilization`: Justice Party / Self-Respect / Dravidian mobilization made caste categories politically actionable;
- `H-A3.2 state_classification`: Indian constitutional and administrative Scheduled Caste/OBC categories created durable instruments unavailable or politically disfavoured in Sri Lanka;
- `H-A3.3 nationalism_caste_blindness`: minority Tamil nationalism in Sri Lanka suppressed open caste articulation to maintain ethnonational unity, producing “caste blindness” rather than redistribution;
- `H-A3.4 welfare_substitution`: universalist Sri Lankan education/welfare reduced some inequalities without caste-targeted policy, changing the political demand for explicit quotas.

Do not rank these before sourcing.

### C20-A4 — War as equalizer, suppressor, or transformer
Did the civil war reduce caste hierarchy, merely silence it, or reconfigure it?

Separate at least four mechanisms:
- LTTE political/military suppression of explicit caste practices;
- recruitment and armed authority disrupting old status relations;
- displacement redistributing land, residence and occupational networks;
- post-war return/reconstruction reactivating caste through housing, IDP camps, temples and marriage.

The question must remain bidirectional: war may weaken some caste practices while worsening material vulnerability for historically oppressed groups.

### C20-A5 — Caste × class × ethnicity
When does a caste explanation add explanatory power beyond class, rurality, region or ethnicity?

Require disconfirming cases. Example: if poverty/displacement patterns disappear once landlessness and wartime displacement are controlled for, do not label the residual mechanism “caste” without evidence.

### C20-A6 — Plantation Tamils are a different caste system
Do not project Jaffna Tamil caste organization onto Malaiyaha/Indian Tamil estate communities. The UN Special Rapporteur literature explicitly notes parallel caste systems among Sinhala, Sri Lankan Tamil and Indian Tamil groups. Link to Run19 `A18_malaiyaha_tamils_status_and_wage`, but preserve the distinction.

### C20-A7 — Temple access as a measurable mechanism
Use temple-entry conflicts and litigation as a high-resolution case study of status change. A candidate anchor is the litigation around the Prevention of Social Disabilities Act and temple access in northern Sri Lanka. Measure:
- legal rule;
- institution enforcing/excluding;
- social response;
- whether access changed status relations outside the temple.

# WORKSTREAM B — Tamil conflict, mass atrocities, memory and the disputed genocide frame

## 6. Input framing

Video `V20-04` is independently indexed under a title equivalent to **“Le Génocide des Tamouls dont personne ne parle”**. That title is itself useful evidence of a contemporary memory/advocacy frame, but **not proof of the legal-historical proposition “Sri Lanka committed genocide against Tamils.”**

The intake must split four objects that public narratives frequently collapse:

1. documented discrimination and anti-Tamil violence across the post-independence period;
2. conduct of the armed conflict, including alleged serious violations by Sri Lankan forces and the LTTE;
3. legal characterization: war crimes, crimes against humanity, genocide;
4. memory politics and diaspora advocacy after 2009.

The repository should be able to explain why the genocide label exists, what evidence its proponents invoke, why major UN accountability findings use other legal categories, and what remains judicially unresolved — without laundering either advocacy or state denial into fact.

## 7. Arc routing

Primary corpus: `sri_lanka_post_1948`.

Likely existing arcs to inspect before allocation:
- citizenship/statelessness and post-1948 minority settlement;
- language/decolonization 1956–1976;
- constitutional centralization / standardization / Tamil federalism and separatism;
- Black July and transition to full-scale war;
- LTTE/state conflict and 2009 endgame;
- post-war militarization, accountability, disappearances, land, diaspora and memory.

**Do not create a single “Tamil genocide” arc.** Route the legal-characterization dispute as a cross-arc research question and a historiography/memory layer.

## 8. Research questions to preregister

### C20-B1 — Sequence, not teleology
Which post-1948 policies and episodes are necessary to explain escalation from parliamentary ethnic conflict to separatism and civil war, without writing the history backward from 2009?

Minimum episodes to test in causal sequence:
- 1948–49 citizenship/disenfranchisement affecting Indian/Malaiyaha Tamils — keep distinct from Sri Lankan Tamil politics;
- 1956 Sinhala Only and subsequent language settlements;
- education/standardization disputes;
- constitutional changes and Tamil federalist/separatist demands;
- recurring communal violence, especially 1958 and 1983;
- militant fragmentation, rise and coercive consolidation of the LTTE;
- Indian intervention / IPKF / regional Tamil Nadu dimension;
- ceasefires and failed settlements;
- 2006–09 endgame.

### C20-B2 — Actor separation
For every atrocity or coercion claim, encode **actor, victim group, date range, geography and evidence type**. Do not let “state versus Tamils” erase:
- LTTE killings, forced recruitment, political assassinations and coercion of Tamil civilians;
- violence against Muslims, including expulsions from the North;
- Sinhala civilian victims;
- intra-Tamil violence;
- distinctions among government, armed forces, paramilitaries and local authorities.

This is necessary both for accuracy and for causal explanation of political consolidation.

### C20-B3 — 2009 casualty and conduct claims
Separate:
- casualty estimates;
- shelling/no-fire-zone allegations;
- hospitals/humanitarian objects;
- deprivation of food/medicine;
- LTTE use of civilians, restrictions on flight and forced recruitment;
- executions, disappearances and detention after surrender.

Each is an atomic evidence problem. No aggregate narrative claim should substitute for them.

### C20-B4 — Genocide: legal test versus political memory
Build a legal-characterization matrix:

| Category | Required research distinction |
|---|---|
| war crimes | nexus to armed conflict + prohibited conduct |
| crimes against humanity | widespread/systematic attack directed against civilian population + knowledge |
| genocide | enumerated acts + specific intent to destroy, in whole or substantial part, a protected national/ethnic/racial/religious group |

Questions:
- Which evidence is invoked to establish **specific genocidal intent**, rather than intent to defeat the LTTE or commit other international crimes?
- Has any competent international or domestic court adjudicated the Sri Lankan case as genocide?
- What did the UN Panel of Experts (2011) and OISL/OHCHR (2015) actually conclude, and what did they not conclude?
- Which genocide allegations in UN repositories are NGO submissions rather than findings of UN organs? Preserve document provenance explicitly.
- How do diaspora commemorations, Tamil political resolutions, state commemorations and battlefield-victory narratives differ in vocabulary and implied causality?

### C20-B5 — State discrimination versus exterminatory intent
Test whether the long-run sequence from language, constitutional and territorial policies to wartime violence supports:
- continuity of ethnic majoritarianism;
- changing mechanisms across periods;
- or a single continuous exterminatory project.

A single 60-year causal line is a **high-risk overclaim** unless intent and mechanism continuity are demonstrated period by period.

### C20-B6 — Spatial dimension
Map at least:
- North/East demographic geography;
- major pogrom/riot locations and displacement corridors;
- LTTE territorial control over time;
- final-war Vanni / Mullivaikkal geography;
- post-war military land occupation / return where evidence supports it;
- diaspora destination nodes only when they explain political memory or remittance networks.

The goal is to prevent a national narrative from flattening regional mechanisms.

### C20-B7 — India/Tamil Nadu as regional actor
Treat Tamil Nadu and the Indian central government separately. Questions:
- refugee flows and Tamil Nadu public opinion;
- Indian training/support to Tamil militant groups in earlier phases;
- 1987 Indo-Sri Lanka Accord and IPKF;
- Rajiv Gandhi assassination and policy reversal;
- post-2009 diaspora/Tamil Nadu memory politics.

This creates a bridge back to Workstream A without conflating caste and ethnonational conflict.

### Provenance warning

UN Digital Library documents titled around “Genocide of Tamils in Sri Lanka” can be **NGO written statements submitted to the Human Rights Council**, e.g. `A/HRC/40/NGO/218`; their presence in a UN repository does **not** make the allegation a UN finding. Encode `issuer_type=NGO`, not `UN_finding`.

## 10. Required drift audit — Workstream B

Flag or reject:

- `Tamil == LTTE`;
- `Sri Lankan state == Sinhala civilians`;
- “civil war began in 1956” without distinguishing political conflict from armed conflict;
- “Black July caused separatism” as single-cause language;
- any exact 2009 death toll presented without provenance/range/method;
- “the UN concluded genocide” unless a specific UN organ/document actually does so;
- NGO statements in UN repositories misrepresented as UN adjudications;
- state denial used to erase credible allegations;
- advocacy labels used as substitutes for legal elements;
- false balance that treats all actors as equivalent merely because violations are alleged on multiple sides;
- teleology: treating every post-1948 event as an inevitable step toward 2009.

# 11. Cross-workstream hypotheses worth testing

These are **questions**, not bridges.

### H20-X1 — Minority nationalism can conceal internal hierarchy
Did the political need to present a unified Tamil minority against Sinhala majoritarianism make intra-Tamil caste inequality less publicly legible, even when it persisted socially?

Potential evidence: political rhetoric, anti-caste movements, LTTE governance, temple disputes, post-war caste research.

### H20-X2 — War destroys some status institutions and freezes others
Displacement, militarization and diaspora may disrupt local patron-client structures while preserving endogamy, kinship and inherited capital. Test domains separately.

### H20-X3 — Diaspora changes both class mobility and memory production
Does diaspora mobility weaken caste in employment/public life while reproducing it in marriage/association networks, and simultaneously strengthen transnational conflict-memory institutions? Do not assume the two processes move together.

### H20-X4 — Tamil Nadu is a comparator and an actor, not a template
Tamil Nadu can explain alternative policy technologies around caste and can act directly in Sri Lankan Tamil politics, but the two functions must be encoded separately.
